## Numerical interpretation

The theta transformation removes the voltage cutoff algebraically. It does not
remove time-step or finite-population effects. The weak-drive theta depths are
0.156 at N=8000 and 0.137 at N=16000, compared with the exact mean-field value
0.128. The half-step and doubled-window depths are 0.155 and 0.151. Only the
baseline settings have a matched field-off run; no mean-rate percentage is
inferred for the other settings. The three old cutoff depths are consistent
with an inverse-cutoff correction, but this fit is not proof of joint
convergence. The largest sampled phase step increases with population size,
so the N and time-step limits cannot be declared independent from these runs.

The access figure uses a 10-Hz beat and exact tone-by-tone point-membrane
attenuation. The reported Missey comparison separately uses the Methods pairs
1300/1430 and 9000/9130 Hz, giving a source ratio of 0.030 at 0.2 ms and equal
local fields. The narrowband 9/1-kHz ratio is 0.020. Its 1/81 asymptote requires
both bands above their access corners; the long-cable asymptote is 1/9.
Neither comparison measures the experiment's local source or network gain.
