\subsection{Static rate with dynamic synapses}
A second-order synapse with time constant $\tau_s$ obeys
\begin{equation}
r\simeq \frac{F(Q)}{\tau_m},\qquad
\tau_s^2\ddot u+2\tau_s\dot u+u=r,
\label{eq:slow_synapse}
\end{equation}
where $u$ is the postsynaptic potential entering the recurrent drive in $Q$, measured in
units of its steady gain: the filter of \S\ref{sec:nmm} with $a=1/\tau_s$ has gain
$A\tau_s$, and dividing its potential by $A\tau_s$ gives this equation \cite{clusella2023}. This is the slow-synapse limit: shortening the
effective membrane time makes the transfer appear static while the network retains
its synaptic dynamics.
