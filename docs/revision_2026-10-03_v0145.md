# v0.14.5: effective weak-field mixing

Giulio authorized this revision after clarifying that the paper concerns weak-field
TI and can combine cellular nonlinearities in an effective quadratic response. The
comparison baseline is v0.14.4 at `2ccea30`; the intervening commits contain review
notes only. Release and circulation remain held.

The abstract and storyline were updated before the manuscript. The paper now states
the weak-field scope in the Introduction and distinguishes smoothness and a nonzero
second-order term from the separate condition of fast relaxation. Theory explains
the QIF rate curve as the population average of thresholded single-neuron square-root
rates. It defines the complex generated-rate coefficient `chi^(2)` at fixed synaptic
input, with cosine-amplitude normalization giving `sigma''/2` in the memoryless case.
Multiple cellular contributions can enter that coefficient; its value and frequency
dependence remain model-specific. This equation is unnumbered, preserving existing
equation references. The added content does not claim an HH calibration.

The Discussion and Conclusion carry the effective-response interpretation. They
separate modulation of ongoing activity from direct carrier-evoked firing and block,
and label finite-amplitude examples as illustrations beyond the expansion rather
than measurements of weak-field physiological thresholds. Plovie's comparison is
scoped to the neuron models it tested. Predictions distinguish QIF's DC coefficient
from its beat response and require nonzero curvature for the static square law.

Cifra et al. (2026), doi:10.1039/D3CS00244F, is cited in the Discussion access
paragraph for the review's exclusion of fields below 0.1 kV/m from its scope. The
clause does not make this a universal biological threshold or a membrane-channel
cutoff. The bibliography entry follows the article's author list and publication
metadata. The efficiency paragraph now says the relative weak-input efficiency
decreases as the input is weakened, matching its formula; this closes R10.

All 22 included figure identities and their order, section headings and order,
equation labels, simulation code, data, and parameters are unchanged. The placement
decisions on Theory, LaNMM methods, and supplementary figures remain open. The
storyline no longer calls JR and QIF the same detector, identifies the DC calculation
and four-point PING check, and marks remaining surrogate/capture/lock-in issues as
review items. Other R1–R13 statuses are not declared closed by this pass.
