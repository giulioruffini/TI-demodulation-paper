\newcommand{\absObjective}{Temporal-interference (TI) stimulation requires field-to-membrane
access, nonlinear generation of a difference-frequency component absent from the
field, and amplification of that component. We connect these stages for weak
perturbations of ongoing activity.}

\newcommand{\absApproach}{Single-neuron TI models already demonstrate axonal ion-channel
rectification. We represent candidate fast access through axonal compartments,
including nodes and the axon initial segment, by a short effective membrane time.
Periaxonal circuitry motivates separate axonal and somatic voltage responses.
We analyze mixing and recurrent gain in a Jansen--Rit
column, an exact quadratic integrate-and-fire mean field, and its spiking network.}

\newcommand{\absResults}{For a smooth response with a nonzero second-order term,
cellular contributions combine into a beat proportional to the product of the
effective carrier amplitudes. A static rate curve mixes through its curvature;
the exact mean field recovers this description when membrane relaxation is fast
relative to the input, leaving dynamic synapses. Otherwise mixing depends on
frequency. Membrane attenuation enters once, through access. Near a Hopf bifurcation,
the network amplifies the generated beat as $1/\gamma$ within linear response,
where $\gamma$ is modal damping. Coupling sweeps separate this gain from leading-order
beat generation. Rate modulation grows faster than the mean-rate change near resonance.
Spiking simulations distinguish beat modulation below the oscillatory threshold from
gamma induced when rectification crosses it. An equal-power mean-field carrier
control separates induction from the beat response.}

\newcommand{\absSignificance}{Fast access, cellular mixing, and slow circuit resonance form
a consistent candidate mechanism; its field-to-voltage transfer and response at
human field strengths require calibration. The product law makes envelope-only
dosimetry conditional on transduction. Within linear response, TI and direct
low-frequency stimulation share the amplifier when coupling, readout, and state
are matched, so resonance alone gives TI no efficiency advantage.}
