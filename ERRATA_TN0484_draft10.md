## Evidence for each finding

### 1. [MAJOR] line 1493, mode-misidentification

> $\bar\eta$ toward the gamma Hopf $J^*$, the demodulated peak of $r_E$ grows as $1/\gamma$ before departing from this scaling near criticality

nmm2_jcD.py takes gamma from gam_w (nmm2_jcA.py), which returns the least-damped eigenvalue with frequency in 25–90 Hz. I re-diagonalized the Jacobian with the paper's own engine at every cached C. The mode that crosses at J*=16.66 sits at 36.5, 39.1, 41.0, 42.5 ... 48.2 Hz for C=13.0...15.815, with damping 0.0549 -> 0.0166 /ms. The cached gamma for those 11 points (0.0487 -> 0.0165 /ms) belongs to a different pair at 47.2 -> 55.4 Hz, whose damping then levels off near 0.017 /ms and never reaches the Hopf (0.01736 /ms at the last point). The selector switches between the two modes at C~15.87 (55.4 -> 48.6 Hz), which shows up in the cache as the fpk jump 56.5 -> 49 Hz. The lock-in grid is Delta f=40–58 Hz, so for C<=14.3 the critical resonance (36.5–42.5 Hz) lies outside the grid or at its edge: fpk=40.0 Hz, the grid boundary, for the first three points. Scaling test: peak*gamma would be constant under 1/gamma. With the plotted gamma it drifts 1.72 -> 1.01 -> 1.29 -> 0.83 -> 1.01 -> 0.54 (normalized to index 20). With the critical-mode gamma it falls monotonically 1.94 -> 0.54. The log-log slope over the first 20 points is 0.79 (plotted) or 0.66 (critical), against 1. Over the full sweep the peak grows 11.2x while 1/gamma grows 35x. For contrast, the JR J-curve product gain*gamma stays at 37–49 for gamma=10.7 -> 0.7 s^-1. This rests the statement that the JR and NMM2 circuits 'share the leading gain scaling' (line 1503) on a mixed-mode abscissa.

### 2. [MAJOR] line 935, definition-not-in-cited-source

> The Population Excitation--Inhibition Index (PEIX) of \cite{ruffini2025cfc} is a related but distinct quantity: the sigmoid gain $\Sig'(v^*)$ normalized to its maximum $\Sig'(v_0)=e_0 \rho/2$ at the inflection, ... \in(0,1]

I fetched the cited preprint (bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.03.19.644090 v3, App. A.1 "PEIX computation"). It defines PEIX by normalizing the slope and states its properties: "(x) ≈ 0 when ⟨V⟩ ≈ V0 (linear regime), (x) > 0 when ⟨V⟩ > V0 (excitatory state), (x) < 0 when ⟨V⟩ < V0 (inhibitory state), \|(x)\| ≈ 1 in the saturated regimes." The paper's quantity is the reverse: it is unsigned, equals 1 at the inflection, and tends to 0 in saturation. At best it is 1 − \|PEIX\| of the cited work. Every downstream use inherits the inversion: "PEIX peaks where the demodulation gain vanishes" (l. 947), Prediction 2's "where PEIX (Eq. peix) peaks" (l. 1658), the notation table's "Σ'-based brain-state index", and App. D's η_det ≃ (ρε/4)√(1−PEIX) with "PEIX = 4s(1−s)". A reader who takes PEIX values from the cited LaNMM paper and applies them here will read the operating point backwards.

### 3. [MAJOR] line 2389, mischaracterized-citation

> existing TI studies of deep targets sweep the \emph{beat} frequency rather than the carrier against a network resonance \cite{esmaeilpour2021,violante2023}, so they neither support nor contradict it.

Esmaeilpour et al. 2021 (Brain Stimul 14:55–65, PMC9382891), Methods: "5 Hz amplitude-modulated waveforms with 0.1-2 kHz carrier frequencies". Results: "Selection of carrier frequency strongly affected sensitivity to amplitude modulation stimulation. Amplitude modulated stimulation with 100 Hz carrier frequency required ∼5 V/m ... 1 kHz carrier frequency ∼60 V/m ... 2 kHz carrier frequency ∼80 V/m". Violante et al. 2023 (PMC10620081): "We applied two sinusoidal currents at 2.005 kHz and 2 kHz (resulting in a Δf envelope frequency of 5 Hz)" and "We chose a Δf of 5 Hz within the theta-band". Neither study sweeps the beat. Esmaeilpour 2021 is a carrier sweep against an ongoing gamma network oscillation, which is the kind of evidence prediction (iii) concerns. Its carriers lie at or above 100 Hz, so it does not test gamma-band carriers directly.

### 4. [MAJOR] line 1754, uncredited-prior-work

> The measurements in Table~\ref{tab:doors} and related models support testing shorter access times, with their TI transfer still to be calibrated. Luff et al.'s pulse-width-modulated TI (PWM-TI) model uses a $1$~ms passive response within a $1$--$100$~ms parameter sweep \cite{luff2024}.

Esmaeilpour et al. 2021 (PMC9382891) built a network model of hippocampal gamma in which "the membrane time constant (τ) was decreased to 1 ms" to reproduce kHz TI data, and reported that "Decreasing the membrane time constant increased the sensitivity of gamma oscillations to both amplitude-modulated and unmodulated kHz electric fields (... τm = 0.4 ms)". It concludes: "The TI sensitivity (current required at the scalp) depends on the neuronal membrane time-constant (e.g. axons) approaching the kHz carrier frequency." This is the reduced-model move of the paper's Abstract and Approach ("We represent candidate fast access through axonal compartments ... by a short effective membrane time"), and Esmaeilpour applies it to a network oscillation. The Discussion paragraph that motivates shorter access times cites Luff PWM-TI and Semenov but not this earlier, more directly relevant network study. Under E21, the contribution should be stated as the quantitative structure (product law, QIF crossover, 1/γ gain), not the fast-access idea itself.

### 5. [MAJOR] line 510, false-absolute-waveform-coverage

> every claim that turns on the waveform is run with genuine two tones ... The AM surrogate is retained for the dense perturbative sweeps in $(\Omega,p)$ and $(\Omega,C)$, which read only the leading $\Omega$ component

The paper's own Eq. (amsur) discussion shows that at matched beat amplitude the m=1 surrogate has DC term (3/4)eps^2, while genuine tones have eps^2. code/timing_not_rate.py uses field()=eps*(1+m cos)*cos with m=1, and it reads the mean of r_P, which is a DC observable, across the (Omega,C) sweep. Far from the Hopf the mean shift comes from the carrier DC term: docs/r7_ti_tacs_pinned_2026-10-05.md gives a TI mean-rate shift of -0.0016 against -0.00003 for source-matched tACS at C=110. So the abstract-level ratio 'Modulation exceeds mean shift by 19x far from the Hopf' is surrogate-specific; at leading order genuine tones would give about 19x0.75, roughly 14x. ti_tacs_pinned_diagnostic.py also uses the AM surrogate, and the text attributes the finite-amplitude ratio change to 'stimulation-induced mean shifts'. code/nmm2_jcD_endpoint_check.py records waveform='AM surrogate' for the claim 'Although a DC-only averaged calculation predicts an unstable endpoint, the full periodically forced solution remains attracting'. Genuine tones would raise the DC source by 4/3 at that endpoint, so this stability claim is untested for the physical waveform.

### 7. [MINOR] line 673, broken-converse
