\newcommand{\absObjective}{Temporal-interference (TI) stimulation applies kilohertz fields with
no difference-frequency component. We develop a mesoscale model with three stages in
series: membrane access, nonlinear detection, and circuit amplification. The weak-field
response factorizes into these stages.}

\newcommand{\absApproach}{Following single-neuron models, we represent access by a short
effective membrane time and analyze detection and amplification in a Jansen--Rit column, an
exact mean field of quadratic integrate-and-fire neurons, and its spiking network. A
realistic head model tests spatial predictions.}

\newcommand{\absResults}{For weak fields, quadratic detection generates a beat proportional
to the product of local carrier polarizations and a signed, DC-like steady source
proportional to their summed squares. Static and exact mean-field detectors share this
product law; membranes slow relative to the input add frequency dependence. With a
published hippocampal montage, changing the current ratio moves the envelope and
mean-square foci in opposite directions but only rescales the beat map at fixed neural
state. Hippocampal mean-square polarization rises by 24\% from 1:1 to 1:3 while envelope
and beat averages fall. Near a Hopf bifurcation, gain grows inversely with modal damping
and is shared with transcranial alternating-current stimulation (tACS). At a fixed
Jansen--Rit operating point, coupling changes gain independently of detection;
amplification appears mainly in beat-locked timing, with little mean-rate change. At
stronger fields, the steady source alone can drive a gamma circuit across its oscillation
threshold, as can an equal-power unmodulated carrier.}

\newcommand{\absSignificance}{Weak-field beat dose follows the carrier-polarization product
rather than the envelope minimum. The accompanying steady source can act at depth, with
sign set by detector curvature rather than carrier polarity. Its magnitude has no strict
interior maximum within tissue regions of uniform conductivity, access, orientation, and
detection. Local-polarization measurements, independent carrier-amplitude variation, and
beat-frequency sweeps across states test access, detection, and amplification,
respectively. Equal-power unmodulated-carrier controls separate steady-source effects from
those requiring the beat.}
