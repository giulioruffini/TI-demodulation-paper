\pagetitle{Problem and theory}{First argument block: Sections 1--2}
A two-tone field carries no power at its beat frequency. Access converts the applied field
into local membrane polarization; nonlinear detection generates the beat; the network selects
and amplifies that generated input. The Introduction develops these stages in that order,
with their mechanisms and evidence. The mesoscale model represents the stages in series,
with carrier-frequency sweeps of membrane polarization testing access, independent-amplitude
sweeps testing detection, and beat-frequency sweeps across operating states testing
amplification. Equal-power carrier controls separate the steady shift from beat-locked
activity. Axonal TI rectification is established in prior models;
short effective membrane times were already tested in a TI gamma-network model
(Esmaeilpour et al., 2021). Our contribution is the separation of mixing from
recurrent gain, the exact QIF mean field, the product law, and the shared TI/tACS amplifier.
Fast axonal compartments are the access hypothesis:
Cohen's periaxonal circuit supports distinct nodal and internodal voltage dynamics; the AIS is a
separate candidate with a high sodium-channel density. Neither observation fixes a TI charging
time. Luff supplies evidence for cellular mixing, while cellular resonance and recurrent
amplification remain distinct. QIF connects the effective weak-input account to a neuron model;
its static transfer is an adiabatic limit, not a consequence of small amplitude alone.
