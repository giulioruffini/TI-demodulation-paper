# Field coupling and population response in the Zhao model

Static review, November 10, 2026. No simulation was run. Public code inspected at commit `cd6a240eba49604230b473440b20a85947884cd6` (August 19, 2023): <https://github.com/OpitzLab/TACS_effects_network/tree/cd6a240eba49604230b473440b20a85947884cd6>. The published article is Zhao et al., *Journal of Neural Engineering* **21**, 026024 (2024), DOI <https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/ad37d9>; the publisher-provided full text was read through ResearchGate. The repository predates the published article, so reproduction should establish correspondence before a new parameter campaign.

## The model already has a cellular field input

The public code represents 800 pyramidal cells and 200 interneurons, each with a soma and dendrite. Both compartments carry NEURON's `extracellular` mechanism. Pyramidal cells have active dendrites; the interneuron dendrite is passive. Excitatory synapses enter the dendrite and inhibitory synapses the soma. The existing model therefore lets Raul retain a physically specified extracellular input and measure the population response without prescribing a membrane shift in advance.

For a sinusoid of amplitude \(E_0\) and unit direction \(\hat{\mathbf e}\), the implemented compartment potential is

\[
V_{e,ic}(t)=-10^{-3}\frac{E_0}{a_p}
\hat{\mathbf e}\cdot\mathbf x_{ic}\sin(2\pi ft),
\]

where \(i\) denotes a neuron, \(c\) its compartment, and \(p\) its population. Coordinates are in micrometers, \(E_0\) in V/m (numerically mV/mm), and \(V_e\) in mV. The dimensionless divisors are `a_PY=5` and `a_IN=1.5`: increasing these variables *reduces* coupling. They should be wrapped by explicit multiplicative gains \(g_p=a_p^{\rm baseline}/a_p\), with zero gain implemented by zeroing the field contribution, rather than division by zero. These variables differ from the morphology-scaling parameters also named `alpha` in the cell classes.

Evidence: `run_2C_tACS_network_par.py` lines 25–40, 60–101, 107–148, 218–260; `mod/xtra.mod` lines 28–35; `stimWaveform.hoc` lines 21–34 and 73–85. The mechanism multiplies a spatial potential by the temporal waveform and points to `e_extracellular`. It does not add a prescribed \(\lambda E\) to the recorded membrane voltage. The code attaches one electrical segment per compartment, assigning its field coordinate from the distal dendrite or proximal soma endpoint (main script 225–236). Preserve this calibrated convention when reproducing the paper; investigate segment-center placement as a separate sensitivity.

The published single-cell calibration matched realistic-cell entrainment at 10 Hz after adjusting morphology, conductances, background drive, and field coupling. It does not establish a frequency-independent polarization coefficient. Methods 2.8 also change the PLV reference: baseline uses endogenous LFP, whereas stimulation uses the imposed waveform. A new study should retain both references in both periods. The published simulations cover 1–30 Hz; their 0.05-ms step and 10-Hz calibration do not validate a kHz extension.

## A measured voltage response and an input coefficient answer different questions

For a lumped two-compartment neuron, define \(V_s,V_d\) as transmembrane voltages and \(g_c\) as the axial conductance. The soma receives the axial current

\[
I_{d\to s}=g_c[(V_d-V_s)+(V_{e,d}-V_{e,s})].
\]

Thus the field supplies opposite currents \(I_{s,E}=g_c\Delta V_e\) and \(I_{d,E}=-g_c\Delta V_e\), where \(\Delta V_e=V_{e,d}-V_{e,s}\). This is a spatially differential forcing. A uniform change of extracellular potential has no effect on this current. Replacing the field by an equal shift of both membrane voltages changes the intervention; a balanced pair of injected currents is the appropriate candidate equivalent for this lumped equation. Its equivalence in the NEURON implementation should be checked, including its extracellular circuit settings.

Around a stable stationary state, a linearized model has

\[
\delta\dot{\mathbf x}=A_J\delta\mathbf x+B\mathbf E,
\qquad
\delta\bar V=C\delta\mathbf x,
\qquad
\chi_{VE}(\omega;J)=C(i\omega I-A_J)^{-1}B.
\]

Here \(\mathbf x\) contains neuronal and synaptic states, \(J\) denotes recurrent parameters, \(B\) specifies the field input, and \(C\) specifies the population voltage readout. The susceptibility \(\chi_{VE}\), with units mV/(V/m), includes recurrence and can depend strongly on \(J\) even when \(B\) is unchanged. Local polarization can also change with the conductance and voltage state generated by the network. Both effects can be measured, but a population voltage/field ratio should be called a response susceptibility rather than silently used as the neural mass's input coefficient.

In a reduced model that already contains recurrence, fitting \(\lambda\) to the full network response and then applying that same response as an input counts network gain twice. Estimate the input representation from isolated or synaptic-replay cells, identify the recurrent model separately, and test their composition on withheld field and network conditions. A current-equivalent coefficient can be estimated as \(\chi_{VE}/\chi_{VI}\), with units nA/(V/m), only for a specified injection site, readout, frequency, and state. A frequency-dependent or readout-dependent ratio rejects a single constant current coefficient; small \(\chi_{VI}\) makes this inversion ill conditioned.

The alpha network is an oscillator. Its response may change phase without a proportionate change in oscillation amplitude, so an infinite-time voltage/field ratio need not define a linear material coefficient. Use a stationary nonoscillating state for the initial susceptibility assay; for the oscillating state retain initial phase, phase response, locking, and amplitude separately. The HH network has no exact MPR closure merely because its population mean is recorded.

## A bounded study for Raul

**Reproduction and measurement.** Freeze the public revision and software versions. Reproduce the baseline and a small set of published 10-Hz conditions before interpreting new coupling estimates. Record transmembrane `v` in soma and dendrite, extracellular potentials, spike times, and synaptic conductances or events. Existing recording specifies only soma `v` and dendritic `ex_xtra` (164–168); ensure population aggregates include every cell rather than the two cells selected for trace plots (176). Reuse connectivity and Poisson realizations across paired field conditions and report variability across at least three independent realizations. Verify selected points at half the integration step.

**Cellular calibration.** First disconnect recurrence and apply the extracellular field to each cell type at a controlled resting state. Then replay the complete field-off synaptic event trains from a network run, including background input, without allowing the stimulated cell to change those trains. Conductance replay retains the baseline shunt and time structure while removing recurrent feedback from the intervention. At 5, 10, 20, and 40 Hz, use signed amplitudes 0, ±0.025, ±0.05, and ±0.1 V/m as an initial local-linearity test; add DC steps with stated settling windows. These are proposed test points, not claims of linearity. Estimate soma and dendrite amplitude and phase, their difference, and firing-rate response. Compare actual fields with the balanced-current equivalent and then with a single-site current surrogate. Fit the simplest input representation that predicts both voltage and rate on withheld frequencies.

**Recurrent response.** Keep morphology, direct coupling, cell counts, connectivity realization, and orientation fixed. Multiply all six recurrent weights by a common factor \(j\in\{0.8,1,1.2\}\), initially leaving background drive unchanged. This maps the total consequence of changing coupling. Perform a second family in which the two background weights are adjusted to restore baseline PY and IN rates, and explicitly report the remaining differences in mean voltage, conductance, intrinsic frequency, and synchrony. Matching firing rates alone does not fix the detector or its input resistance. For sharper attribution, repeat the replay calibration separately at each operating state.

Start the network sweep at 5, 10, and 20 Hz with 0 and ±0.05 V/m, plus one doubled-amplitude check at each \(j\). Use additional frequencies near a detected resonance only after this pilot resolves uncertainty. There is no need for a full Cartesian sweep over every parameter. Compare stimulation at the same absolute frequency and at the same detuning from each condition's measured intrinsic frequency; these answer different questions. The existing `nettype` presets change several weights and background inputs together (`NetWeight.py`), so they are useful reference states but not controlled \(J\) perturbations.

**Mechanism tests.** At informative pilot points, vary excitation and inhibition separately by ±20%, holding the other group fixed; next separate the four directed connection classes and the AMPA/NMDA split if needed. Run field-to-PY only, field-to-IN only, and both, with direct gains 0, 1, and 2. Rotate the field by 0°, 45°, and 90° while holding cell orientations fixed. The default interneuron code samples polar angle uniformly (main script 150–153), which is random but not isotropic on a sphere. Preserve it for replication and use uniform \(\cos\theta\) for a separately labeled isotropic control. A heterogeneous population's signed average can cancel while individual polarizations remain appreciable; report both complex population means and distributions of cellular amplitudes.

**Model reduction.** Fit the direct input kernel on the cellular/replay data and the unforced recurrent dynamics on separate network data. Test the combined reduced model against voltage, firing-rate modulation, and phase on network conditions withheld from fitting. A scalar coefficient is adequate only over the stated states, frequencies, and outputs; otherwise retain separate PY/IN inputs, frequency-dependent kernels, or a soma–dendrite variable. A kHz/TI extension is a later experiment requiring access calibration, time-step convergence, and an equal-RMS single-carrier control.

## Estimands and stopping criteria

For each population \(p\), use the signed arithmetic mean \(\bar V_p=N_p^{-1}\sum_i V_i\), keeping soma and dendrite separate. Record both the raw mean and a prespecified subthreshold analysis; spikes can change the raw voltage mean through their timing or count. A spike-masking rule changes the observable and must be fixed before comparisons. Do not average absolute amplitudes and call the result the population voltage.

For an input \(E_0\cos\omega t\), define the ensemble-averaged odd response \(D_p(t)=[\langle\bar V_p^{+}(t)\rangle-\langle\bar V_p^{-}(t)\rangle]/2\). Over an integer number of periods \(T\), estimate

\[
\widehat\chi_p(\omega)=\frac{2}{TE_0}\int_0^T D_p(t)e^{-i\omega t}\,dt.
\]

The even response \([\langle\bar V_p^{+}\rangle+\langle\bar V_p^{-}\rangle]/2-\langle\bar V_p^{0}\rangle\) separates DC and second-harmonic effects from the odd response. Paired random streams reduce variance but do not guarantee that stimulated and unstimulated nonlinear trajectories remain aligned. Use repeated realizations and varied stimulus-onset phases. Retain spike–stimulus PLV, spike–LFP PLV, LFP power, rate modulation, and mean rate as distinct outputs.

Predeclare a provisional 10% tolerance for amplitude dependence and held-out complex-response error, with an absolute noise floor for responses near zero. Require the timestep and sampling uncertainty to be smaller than that tolerance. If the fitted input changes across outputs, frequencies, or network states beyond uncertainty, reject the constant-\(\lambda\) model for that domain. If phase locking makes the response fail to scale linearly with field, report a phase/entrainment response rather than extrapolating a divergent susceptibility. Failure of a scalar reduction is a result, not a reason to fit a new scalar at every condition.
