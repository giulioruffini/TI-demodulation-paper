# v0.14.3: fast membrane, cellular mixing, and recurrent amplification

This local revision implements Giulio's request to shorten the TI paper, connect the
access discussion to the NMM2 slow-synapse limit, and add Nir Grossman as a coauthor.
The comparison baseline is v0.14.2 at commit `538930d`. Release, GitHub push,
Overleaf synchronization, and circulation remain held for Giulio's review.

## The argument follows the time scales

The theory now derives the static QIF population curve and gives the slow-synapse
reduction with physical rate restored: `r ≈ F(Q)/tau_m`. The synapses remain
dynamic. Fast relaxation relative to synapses permits this reduction; applying the
static curve to a carrier also requires `omega_c * tau_rel << 1`. The manuscript
therefore distinguishes the population relaxation time from the nominal membrane
time and does not treat a 10–100-fold reduction as sufficient in every regime.

The QIF curve is nonlinear without recurrent coupling. Its reduction includes QIF
spike generation and the excitability distribution, but does not automatically
include the sodium-channel kinetics measured by Luff and colleagues. A calibrated
dynamic population functional can represent those mechanisms. The text separates
static curvature, dynamic mixing, linear readout, and recurrent amplification, with
each physical filter counted once. The receiver factorization now specifies the
source and readout for both JR and QIF.

The access discussion distinguishes Cohen's periaxonal nanocircuit and approximately
1-ms minimum fitted axonal decay from a measured transfer under an extracellular
TI field. Luff's 1-ms RC example and Semenov's submicrosecond field-induced charging
refer to different models or response modes. The illustrative 0.2-ms access filter
is labeled as an assumption. An additional sodium-gating filter requires its own
justification and must not duplicate dynamics already represented in the model.
Raul's unpublished WP0242 manuscript remains cited for cellular frequency
selectivity and its conductance dependence. No new periaxonal model is required.

The plotted simulations retain their finite membrane times and original parameters.
The slow-synapse expression is an analytic limit, not a claim that these simulations
were rerun in that limit. All 22 figure files and their order are preserved.

## Repetition and unsupported generalizations were removed

The abstracts, Introduction, access discussion, Discussion, predictions, and
Conclusion were shortened. Repeated accounts of mixing and resonance, extended
cross-frequency-coupling history, and speculative access claims were reduced.
The retained conclusions distinguish beat generation from amplification and explain
why shared resonance alone gives TI no efficiency advantage over matched direct
low-frequency stimulation.

The cubic Hopf normal-form appendix now states the result supported by the weak-input
expansion: there is no quadratic difference-frequency source. It no longer asserts
the absence of every higher-order or strong-drive response. The written PING source
normalization was corrected to distinguish a source in `tau_E * dv_E/dt` from one in
`dv_E/dt`; the code already used the correct normalization. The weak-input TI/direct
stimulation amplitude ratio now states the matched amplitudes and uses the absolute
curvature. The prediction that synaptic blockade eliminates all demodulation was
replaced by a prediction about loss of recurrent amplification, allowing cellular
mixing to persist.

Using `texcount` on the source from Introduction through Code and data availability,
including headings and captions, the count falls from 15,041 to approximately
10,592 words (about 30%). This excludes the abstract, bibliography, and appendices.
The article falls from 36 to 30 pages; the combined technical note falls from 52
to 46 pages. The supplement remains 18 pages.
