\pagetitle{Problem and theory}{First argument block: Sections 1--2}
A two-tone field carries no power at its beat frequency. Access converts the applied field
into local membrane polarization; nonlinear detection generates the beat; the network selects
and amplifies that generated input. The Introduction should develop these stages in that order,
with their proposed mechanisms and evidence. Fast axonal compartments are the access hypothesis:
Cohen's periaxonal circuit supports distinct nodal and internodal voltage dynamics; the AIS is a
separate candidate with a high sodium-channel density. Neither observation fixes a TI charging
time. Luff supplies evidence for cellular mixing, while cellular resonance and recurrent
amplification remain distinct. QIF connects the effective weak-input account to a neuron model;
its static transfer is an adiabatic limit, not a consequence of small amplitude alone.
